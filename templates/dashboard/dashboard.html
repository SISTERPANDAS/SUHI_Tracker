Core Thermal Indices & Urban Dynamics Guide
Scroll for expanded details ↓

As cities grow, human infrastructure replaces natural landscapes with concrete, asphalt, and buildings. This drastically alters local microclimates and creates unique heat patterns. To explore these transformations, our platform tracks multi-layered thermal and spatial dynamics over extended historical periods:

SUHI Intensity (Surface Urban Heat Island Intensity)

This measures the temperature difference (ΔT) between densely built-up city centers and cooler, surrounding rural baseline areas. High intensity values mean the city is trapping a lot of heat, usually due to low-albedo (dark or heat-absorbing) construction materials.

LULC Classification (Land Use / Land Cover)

This categorizes every pixel of satellite imagery into four distinct color-coded environmental features: Water bodies (blue), Healthy vegetation (green), Impervious built-up surfaces (red), and Barren/exposed land (yellow).

LST Retrieval (Land Surface Temperature)

Instead of checking standard air temperature, this measures the actual "skin" temperature of the Earth's surface using thermal infrared satellite bands. It accounts for atmospheric conditions and vegetation cover to give an accurate reading of ground heat.

Advanced Mathematical & Spatial Metrics Tracked

To give you deep insights into urban growth, the platform automatically calculates several advanced indicators behind the scenes:

•
Annual Growth Rate (AGR): Measures the percentage expansion speed at which built-up and concrete zones expand year-over-year.
•
Centroid Migration: Tracks the directional shift of the city's geographical center of gravity over decades to see which direction the city is expanding the fastest.
•
Pixel-Level Correlation (r): Evaluates the direct statistical link between increasing impervious surfaces (concrete/buildings) and rising local surface temperatures.
•
Linear Regression (R²): Uses historical urban sprawl metrics to model and predict future temperature trends.
Key Takeaway: By combining high-resolution land cover mapping with multi-temporal temperature timelines, urban planners can easily pinpoint vulnerable heat pockets and design data-driven cooling and mitigation policies.

What is Landsat?

The Landsat Program is the longest-running enterprise for the acquisition of satellite imagery of Earth. Operated jointly as a partnership between NASA and the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), Landsat launched its first satellite (Landsat 1) in July 1972. Together, the series of satellites provides the world's most continuous space-based record of how our planet's land surface, cities, forests, and water bodies have changed over the decades.

Pixel Resolution & Bands: How Satellites "See" Heat and Land

Landsat satellites use specialized multispectral optical and thermal sensors that capture different wavelengths of light—many of which are invisible to the human eye.

30-Meter Pixel Size:

Most standard optical bands (like Red, Green, Blue, and Near-Infrared) capture data at a spatial resolution of 30 meters. This means every single pixel in the image represents a 30m × 30m square on the ground (roughly the size of a baseball infield).

Catching Light and Heat:
  • Optical Bands: Calculate light reflectance. For example, combining Red and Near-Infrared (NIR) bands allows us to compute the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) to measure plant health and density.
  • Thermal Infrared Bands: Measure the actual thermal radiation (heat energy) emitted upward from ground surfaces, which is the foundation for tracking urban heat islands.
Flexible Region Selection Methods

When starting your urban heat and expansion analysis, you need to tell the platform where you want to look. You can easily define your study area using any of these three beginner-friendly methods:

1. Major City Dropdown

What it is: A pre-configured list of major Indian metropolitan areas and urban centers.

How to use it: Simply open the dropdown menu, select a city (such as Hyderabad), and the platform will automatically load its boundary and geographic coordinates so you can start analyzing right away.

2. Coordinate Inputs

What it is: A precise, numbers-based location finder.

How to use it: Type in the exact Longitude and Latitude values of a specific location. The platform will automatically generate a center buffer zone around those coordinates to target your exact local region of interest.

3. Custom Drawing

What it is: An interactive map tool that lets you draw your own boundaries.

How to use it: Use the map drawing toolbar on the dashboard to click and outline custom polygons or rectangles around any exact neighborhood, district, or study zone you want to investigate.

How Analysis Works (Step-by-Step Workflow)
1. Login & Select Region of Interest (ROI)

Choose between Indian major cities dropdown, coordinate points, or custom drawing tools on the map selector.

2. Timeline & Interval Selection
